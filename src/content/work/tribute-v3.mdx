Tribute's group video product had grown by accretion over six years. Every new
feature had bolted another step onto the invite flow, and the result was a
product that tested well with people who already understood it and lost
everyone else.

## Where it broke down

Session replays showed the same drop-off in three places: choosing an occasion,
inviting contributors, and the moment of first record. Each screen individually
tested fine. The sequence did not.

<PullQuote cite="Head of Growth, Tribute">
  We had been optimising the steps. Nobody had questioned whether the steps
  needed to exist.
</PullQuote>

## What changed

The redesign collapsed the first three screens into one and deferred every
decision that could be made later. Occasion selection became a default with an
edit affordance rather than a blocking choice.

- Invite and record became parallel paths instead of sequential gates
- The design system dropped from 340 components to 128
- Every surface moved onto a single fluid type ramp
