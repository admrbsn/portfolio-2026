The recording flow is the core interaction on Tribute: it is where participants
contribute the video messages that become the final product. Over twenty million
submissions have passed through it. It was also the most fragile part of the
platform, held back by a dated interface, an unreliable recorder built on Ziggeo
and an aging CDN, poor mobile behavior, and no way to submit anything but video.

## The problem, split in two

Aggregate data showed a 33% submission rate, but the average hid two different
failures. Some people abandoned at the very first recording screen, before they
had committed anything. Others started recording and then hit a technical failure
mid-process. Treating those as one number would have led to one fix; separating
them shaped the whole redesign.

A PostHog funnel made it concrete: 79% of users moved past the initial screen, but
only 42% completed submission, for an overall 33% conversion. Mobile dropped off
harder at every stage, and long processing waits correlated with abandonment.

<PullQuote cite="Customer research, recording flow">
  The friction was not only technical. For many people, recording your own face
  and trying to be sincere is a genuinely vulnerable thing to do.
</PullQuote>

## Designing for hesitation, not just uptime

Research surfaced real emotional resistance, especially among introverts,
independent of any bug. Prototype testing in Maze validated the redesigned flow
and pointed to one clear request: people wanted a visible progress indicator
showing where they were and what was left. Adding it measurably reduced hesitation
in later rounds. Rather than jumping to pixel-perfect mockups, we prototyped in
Bolt.new to validate the flow logic before committing to visuals.

## Rebuilding the foundation

The second objective was to remove the reliability problems at the source. The new
flow runs on Vue and Tailwind with Pinia for state and FormKit for forms,
VideoRecord-js for capture, Vimeo for resumable uploads, Backblaze and Bunny for
photos, and Stripe for the gift-card integration. On iOS and Android, native HTML
input fields replaced the custom recorder, which eliminated a whole class of
platform-specific bugs.

Smaller touches carried a lot of the experience: selected Tribute themes appear as
dynamic backdrops so recording feels connected to the celebration, inline
checkmarks confirm each input, hovering record disables upload (and vice versa) to
show they are two paths to the same outcome, a hidden notes field acts like a
teleprompter to cut down retakes, and a confetti finish rewards completion.

## Results

The redesign only worked because it treated infrastructure reliability as a
conversion feature in its own right. People who hit a failed upload or a long
processing delay rarely came back, which made backend stability inseparable from
trust. The one thing I would do differently is treat resumable background uploads
as a day-one requirement rather than a stretch goal, given how many people record
on unreliable mobile connections.
