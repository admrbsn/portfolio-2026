Sixty percent of Peterson's test-prep users already studied on mobile, despite a
barely functional responsive experience. As direct-to-consumer sales climbed from
$50K in 2017 toward roughly $1M by 2021, leadership decided in Q2 2020 to build
native iOS and Android apps. I joined as design lead after an outsourced designer
departed, inheriting a four-month deadline.

## Who we were designing for

Peterson's 185 courses served very different learners: 16 to 18-year-olds taking
the ACT and SAT, active-duty military preparing for the ASVAB, and mid-career
professionals pursuing licensing like real estate. The core problem was blunt.

<PullQuote cite="Peterson's research">
  Test prep on mobile is hard. Complex math, long reading passages, and
  interactive content simply don't render well on a phone.
</PullQuote>

Competitors like Duolingo and Masterclass succeeded with simpler content; Peterson's
needed to handle breadth. A SWOT teardown of Coursera showed functional but
uninspired design, syncing issues, and heavy reliance on webviews that felt
disconnected from native. Three personas anchored the work: June, a 16-year-old
studying in short bursts; David, an Army servicemember needing offline access; and
Frank, a 40-year-old electrician syncing across phone and computer.

## Framing the build

The approach paired native iOS and Android with Auth0 single sign-on to sync
progress across devices, webview-delivered course content to stay in sync with the
LMS, a rewards and badge system for engagement, and strict adherence to Apple's
Human Interface Guidelines and Android's Material Design. Wireframes iterated
through daily design calls and testing with three participants before moving to
high fidelity, with the design system built in Sketch and exported through Zeplin,
using Inter and full light and dark modes.

## The hard parts

Two rounds of testing on UserTesting.com validated the direction: the Auth0 flow
drew praise for smoothness, and people found account creation and course discovery
intuitive. The constraints came from course content delivered through D2L
Brightspace webviews, where cross-origin policies blocked styling control and dark
mode failed inside the webview.

Both problems came down to code. I wrote custom JavaScript and CSS to make the
Auth0 login match native behavior closely enough that testers never flagged it.
For the webviews, user-agent detection served app-specific stylesheets, and CSS
custom properties plus `prefers-color-scheme` restored dark mode despite
Brightspace's limitations.

## Outcomes

The apps shipped inside the four-month window and supported Peterson's growth. In
hindsight, Brightspace's iframe architecture constrained design freedom throughout,
and negotiating rendering control during planning would have been worth the
schedule cost. The personas held up well: June's chip-away pattern shaped the
reward system, David's offline needs shaped content caching, and Frank's
cross-device requirement validated the Auth0 architecture.
