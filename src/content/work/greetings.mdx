Tribute's group-video product had proven that multimedia changes how people
respond to a message. Greetings took that insight into a new category: one-to-one
digital gifting, pairing a personal video message with a curated gift. It launched
across web, iOS, and Android within six to eight months, built by two designers
and a small engineering team.

## The problem

Existing digital greetings and gift cards felt transactional and emotionally flat.
The opportunity was to bring Tribute's multimedia warmth to a much simpler
interaction, without dragging along the complexity of the group-video flow.

## The shape of the product

Three features carried the concept. Curated gifts, from gift cards to flowers to
artisanal products, gave more personality than a standard e-gift card. Prompt
cards answered the hesitation people feel in front of a blank text field. And
group gifting let several contributors add to a single greeting, preserving some
of the collaborative energy of the original product.

## Research and restraint

I ran research with 300 participants across moderated and unmoderated sessions
using Lookback and PlaybookUX, synthesizing in Dovetail. People wanted one-to-one
gifting alongside the group model, consistently preferred clean, uncluttered
interfaces, and cared about scheduling and dual email or SMS delivery.

Unmoderated, task-based testing in Maze then delivered the most useful finding:
audio features and social integrations were not valued. That pushed the roadmap
toward cuts rather than additions, removing social options and deprioritizing
audio.

<PullQuote cite="Reflection, Greetings">
  The strongest version was the simplest. Cutting features strengthened the core
  experience rather than weakening it.
</PullQuote>

## Design and build

Both primary flows stayed deliberately linear. Sending walked people through
recipient details, message creation, gift selection, and scheduling. Receiving
used a story-player format familiar from Instagram and Snapchat. Crazy Eights kept
ideation fast inside tight constraints.

The team built cross-platform with Ionic, Vue, and Tailwind, reusing existing
authentication and marketplace code to save time. Web shipped first under the
Tribute domain to avoid app-store delays, followed by iOS with native
authentication adjustments, then Android. The lasting lesson was restraint:
validating earlier would have surfaced the case for simplicity sooner.
