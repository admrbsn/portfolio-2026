I joined Tribute in late March 2020, just as the pandemic sent demand for group
video montages sharply upward. Revenue went from $250K in 2019 to $700K in a
single month by May 2020, with a million videos created. The backend held up. Almost
everything users touched, and the design organization meant to improve it, did not
yet exist in any structured form.

## The problem

User research kept returning the same three pain points. New visitors could not
tell what the product was. Pricing was unclear about what you paid for and when.
And the recording flow, the heart of the product, was dated, unreliable, and
barely usable on mobile.

<PullQuote cite="User research, 2020">
  The homepage didn't explain the product. New visitors couldn't tell what
  Tribute was or how it worked.
</PullQuote>

## Building the system before the screens

The strategy was clarity, simplicity, and reliability, but none of it could hold
without infrastructure to support it. I established a shared design system in
Figma, stood up GA and PostHog for funnel visibility, set up continuous A/B
testing through Google Optimize, and put a regular customer-feedback cadence in
place.

To do the work, I hired two full-time designers and brought in a small NYC design
agency, then ran weekly design sprints using Crazy Eights, affinity mapping, and
structured "spicy debates" that forced the team to articulate tradeoffs out loud.
We bootstrapped the design system from Pegasus and tested prototypes in Maze, with
designers working alongside engineers through implementation and A/B testing after
launch.

## Moving to Vue 3

The responsive interactivity we wanted was not achievable inside Django's
server-side rendering. We migrated to Vue 3, using Vite to embed Vue apps into the
existing Django structure so we could prototype and deploy independently.
Decoupling frontend from backend let the two ship on their own schedules and made
the whole product easier to maintain.

Checkout conversion rose 16% and average order value climbed about $30 alongside
those gains. This was the project that set the foundation for everything Tribute
has shipped since; the real work was building the design infrastructure, because
no system, analytics, testing process, or full-time team existed when I arrived.
My one regret is waiting as long as I did to move to Vue, after months spent
pushing against the limits of the Django-rendered frontend.
